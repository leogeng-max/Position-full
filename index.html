Prototype Versions

Issues With Circles

  • Numbers don't fit. Circle size follows the data, so a small bucket can't hold a label like "−6.1pp".
  • Area is misread. People underestimate area (Cleveland & McGill); 2× the area looks ~1.4× bigger.
  • Size meaning was ambiguous. The original text said "total" capital; the math used the average.
  • Two metrics in one shape. Readers assume the bigger circle "won", which isn't necessarily true.
  • Can conflict with Calibration if the same data is shown two different ways.

How Version B Solves Them

  • Contribution sits below the circle, large and in green/red. The circle never has to fit it.
  • Inside the circle is only what its size means — the average initial weight (e.g. 4.2%), short enough to always fit.
  • Area-true sizing with a floor: circle area ∝ average weight; the smaller circle never drops below 60% of the larger's diameter.
  • Explicit label: "Circle area = average initial weight" and "N positions" under each circle.
  • Net result stays explicit in the Net Effect / Positions / Record Maturity rows.
  • Tap opens Calibration, where the same numbers appear in the precise cards and bridge: circles for the glance, cards for the analysis.

What Changed in C

  • Top bar: real status bar plus an app bar (date line, page title, theme switch), same structure as A/B.
  • Muted colours: green #2CAC55, red #DE3834, gold #C29A2A — the same quieter palette as B.
  • No all-caps: titles and labels in Title Case; section titles follow A (15px, primary ink, info icon beside the title).
  • Square corners on every box; circles stay round.
  • Left-aligned content throughout; nothing centred.
  • More air: larger gaps between sections, inside cards and between rows; one 20px side gutter.
  • Readable type: nothing below 10px (the original went down to 7.5px); circles large enough for their numbers.
  • Watchlist → Under Review, same row pattern as the main dashboard.

Unchanged

  • Dave's layout and card structure, circle design with the number inside, bottom tab bar, light/dark themes, all content, data and interactions.

Key Findings
Initial Allocation vs Outcome

Two cards, two channels. The bar shows either the average initial weight of positions in that bucket (Capital mode) or the average number of Capital Events per position (Activity mode) — never a sum, since positions were entered at different times against different account totals and adding their percentages together would describe a portfolio that never existed at any single moment. The large number is always the same regardless of mode — that bucket's contribution to this diagnostic, in percentage points — so switching modes changes what the bar means, never what the number means. This is a cross-sectional statistic — initial size weighted against final outcome — not a time-weighted account return. Current descriptive read, not the account's Outcome or a verified Decision Alpha.

Hit Rate & Payoff, by Behavior

How often each type of capital move was followed by the outcome it implies — an add "right" means the security kept outperforming afterward; a trim or exit "right" means the security kept underperforming afterward. Hit rate alone can mislead: a move that's right less than half the time can still be a net positive if the wins are large enough, and a move that's right more than half the time can still be a net negative if the misses are large enough. The magnitude comparison below each hit rate is what actually determines that, not the percentage alone. Exits are split by whether the position was ahead or behind the benchmark at the moment of sale — cutting a loser and taking profit on a winner are different decisions, and the record may show a different pattern for each.

Why

Every element driving the result above, in the order decisions occur: selection, initial sizing, adds and trims, exit — then three summary reads (payoff, loss containment, holding). Tap any row for the underlying figures. Hit rate alone is insufficient — capital size, payoff magnitude, and subsequent adaptation determine its economic consequence, which is why selection is listed first but its hit-rate framing shouldn't be read as the primary number on this page.

It never overrides the Capital Calibration result above; it adds detail only for the subset of positions carrying a Forecast, and remains optional otherwise.
